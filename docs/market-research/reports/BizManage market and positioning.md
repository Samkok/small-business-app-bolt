# Sell shop owners their missing money

BizManage's paying customer is not the typical micro shop but the one that has just outgrown it: a shop that has hired someone outside the family, opened a second branch, or started buying stock by the carton and importing it, so the owner can no longer tell from a notebook or a bank app where the money and the goods went. That buyer is a minority of a very large base. Cambodia alone has about **754,000 establishments, two-thirds of them in trade and 96.5% sole proprietorships** ([NIS Economic Census 2022](https://nis.gov.kh/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/National-Report-on-Final-Census-Results-Economic-Census-of-Cambodia-2022.pdf)), with a median of two workers ([ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)). For exactly this buyer, the competition charges per employee, per store or per add-on for the features that matter (staff roles, inventory with cost of goods, unit conversion, landed cost). BizManage includes all of them for **$9.99 a month for the whole team**, a price that sits at the market rate for a single branch in Southeast Asia and far below a stacked Loyverse, Square or Shopify setup. The opening is real: no competitor's title or tagline claims "real profit after cost of goods", the local App Store has no POS app with more than about 30 local ratings, and no phone-first app under $10 combines a Khmer interface, two currencies, carton-to-piece units, landed cost and unlimited staff. The obstacles are BizManage's own. The listing is English-only, has zero ratings and still shows two retired plans. The 50-sale free tier risks a paywall before the owner has seen a profit number. *(Corrected 15 Nov 2026: the free cap is 50 sales, not 10. This judgement was made at 10 and is weaker at 50.)* And owners search for "track stock", "daily sales" and "sales and inventory", never for the differentiator words in BizManage's copy. The positioning that follows from the evidence is "real profit and stock you can trust, for the whole team, on one phone", told in owners' words and, in every public surface, without naming any country.

## Half a million shops need it; the ones with staff will pay

The need is broad and mostly unmet. Wholesale and retail trade makes up **65.6% of Cambodian establishments**, which works out to roughly 494,000 trade businesses (my calculation from the census share) ([NIS EC2022](https://nis.gov.kh/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/National-Report-on-Final-Census-Results-Economic-Census-of-Cambodia-2022.pdf)), and about 81% of MSMEs sit outside the capital ([ADB Asia SME Monitor 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1096981/asia-sme-monitor-2025.pdf)). Payments have gone digital: **74% of small firms receive e-payments** ([Asia Foundation CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)), and KHQR volume passes $105 billion a year ([Cambodia Investment Review](https://cambodiainvestmentreview.com/2026/06/08/exclusive-cambodias-digital-economy-reaches-new-milestone-as-mobile-banking-users-surpass-10-million-and-khqr-payments-exceed-105-billion-annually/)). Back-office tools have not followed. An ADB key informant put it plainly: owners use digital tools for payments and marketing, but using apps "such as stock management, is limited". Meanwhile **78% of micro owners say their business needs more digital tools** ([ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)). The same gap shows up across the region. In Viet Nam, the most mature micro-retail software market, KiotViet's claimed 300,000 stores against about 1.9 million retail shops implies roughly 15% POS penetration (my estimate from company figures) ([KiotViet](https://about.kiotviet.vn/?p=260); [e27](https://e27.co/our-main-barrier-to-growth-is-status-quo-in-retail-sector-kiotviets-deputy-gm-tri-cao-20200605/)). Southeast Asia's mobile internet penetration reached **76% at end-2025** ([GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/1-2-billion-people-across-developing-asia-pacific-remain-offline-despite-near-universal-mobile-coverage/)), so a phone-only product fits the hardware owners already have. What holds adoption back is habit and time. **72% of micro owners spend no time at all on business skills on a typical day** ([ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)).

Need is not the same as willingness to pay, and the base is dominated by people who will struggle to pay. **49% of Cambodian firms are one-person operations**, about 70% are women-owned (74% in sales), and women-owned firms rely more on unpaid family labour ([ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)). Demand is weak: **83% named declining sales as a top concern**, and most firms without a loan say they do not need one ([CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)). A family stall with no outside staff gets most of what it needs from free tools. ABA Merchant gives real-time sales, 30-day reports and cashier assignment at no cost ([ABA Bank](https://ababank.com/aba-merchant-app/)), and Loyverse's free core includes POS, essential inventory and multi-store ([Loyverse pricing](https://loyverse.com/pricing)). Free ledgers elsewhere won adoption, not revenue. Khatabook and BukuWarung grew to millions of merchants and moved toward lending and payments ([Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2021/06/11/sme-bookkeeping-start-up-bukuwarung-bags-60m-series-a-funding.html); [vmobify](https://vmobify.com/blog/b2b-sme-app-marketing-strategy)), and Indonesia's BukuKas shut down in 2023 ([DealStreetAsia](https://media.dealstreetasia.com/stories/indonesia-lummo-liquidation-360554)).

The payer appears once the shop crosses a threshold that free tools charge for. Employee theft accounts for **29% of US retail shrink** ([NRF/Appriss NRSS 2023](https://info.apprissretail.com/hubfs/Resource-Center/2023.038-national-retail-security-survey-report-2023.pdf)). No equivalent figure exists for Southeast Asia, but the problem is typed into search: "how to stop staff stealing" completes to "from till" and "stock" ([Google Suggest](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&q=how%20to%20stop%20staff%20stealing&hl=en&gl=kh)). Loyverse puts staff accountability behind its paid Employee Management add-on and sells a fraud-alert app on top ([Loyverse marketplace](https://loyverse.com/marketplace/pos-detective)), which shows that buyers pay for it. Men own a larger share of the bigger firms (women own only 41% of firms with 5-9 workers), and those firms are more often registered ([ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf); [CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)), so the paying segment is also demographically different from the median shop. Tax is not a trigger in this market. Only **7% of firms are registered with the tax department and about 1% of unregistered firms plan to register** ([CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)), and CamInvoice e-invoicing is mandatory only for government suppliers so far ([EDICOM](https://edicomgroup.com/electronic-invoicing/cambodia)). The pitch has to be money found, not compliance.

The ceiling this implies is modest, and that is worth saying plainly. On my own illustrative assumptions, 1% paid penetration of Cambodia's trade establishments is about 5,000 shops, or roughly $600,000 a year at current prices, and 5% would be about $3 million. Viet Nam and Indonesia are 10-100 times larger by count, but they are held by funded local incumbents that sell through field teams and are already giving away tax-compliant tools during Viet Nam's 2026 shift away from lump-sum tax for about five million household businesses ([Thoi bao Tai chinh](https://thoibaotaichinhvietnam.vn/five-million-household-businesses-to-adopt-self-declared-tax-system-188612.html); [VCCI](https://www.vcci.com.vn/news/kiotviet-mien-phi-phan-mem-ke-toan-cho-ho-kinh-doanh)). The analyst figures of $12-16 billion for global POS software ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/pos-software-market)) are dominated by North American enterprise spend and say nothing useful about phone-only micro retail.

## Six personas, two of which carry the business

The table summarises the personas. Segment sizes and ability to pay are inferred from the census, survey and competitor evidence above, not from a willingness-to-pay study. No such study exists for this market.

| Persona | Who buys, who uses | Trigger moment | Today's tools | Ability to pay $9.99 | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. The owner who just hired help** (minimart, phone accessories, cosmetics, clothing, hardware; 3-10 workers) | Owner buys (often a woman in her 30s-50s, time-poor); hired staff and relatives use it | First non-family hire; owner steps away; a stock count reveals missing goods | Notebook, memory, bank app history | Medium-high | **Primary** |
| **B. The two-shop owner** (absentee or multi-branch) | Owner buys; branch staff use it | Second branch opens; cash and stock at a branch stop matching | Phone calls, Telegram photos of notebooks | High | **Primary** |
| **C. The small importer or wholesaler** | Owner (more often male, larger firm) | A shipment whose true unit cost is unclear; selling by piece what was bought by carton | Excel, notebook | High per account, fewer firms | Secondary |
| **D. The chat and COD seller** (Facebook, TikTok, Telegram) | Owner, sometimes one helper | Orders in chats and courier COD remittances stop reconciling | Chat threads, spreadsheet | Low-medium | Secondary |
| **E. The solo family stall** | Owner alone or with family | Rarely triggered | Notebook, KHQR, bank app | Low | Free-tier only |
| **F. The café or small F&B outlet** | Owner | Needs tables, modifiers, kitchen tickets | Loyverse free POS and kitchen display | Medium | Poor fit |

Personas A and B carry the business because their trigger is one the competition monetises and BizManage gives away. For a shop with three staff that needs inventory with cost, Loyverse runs to about $45 a month with add-ons, and Shopify POS with staff roles costs about $128 ([Loyverse pricing](https://loyverse.com/pricing); [Shopify POS pricing](https://www.shopify.com/pos/pricing)). Sources disagree on whether Loyverse's employee add-on is $5 per employee or $25 per store, which changes the total but not the conclusion. At two branches and five staff, the gap widens to roughly $95 a month for Loyverse against BizManage's flat $9.99 (my calculation from the published add-on prices). Persona C is the importer whose margin depends on shipping being inside the unit cost. That buyer is underserved everywhere: unit-of-measure conversion sits in Zoho Inventory's $129 Premium tier and landed cost in Lightspeed's $89-a-month product ([Zoho Inventory pricing](https://www.zoho.com/inventory/pricing/); [Lightspeed Retail pricing](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/pos/retail/pricing/)). Persona D is large in number. Cambodian online selling runs through Facebook, TikTok and Telegram ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/02/22/booming-platform-economy-offers-convenience-to-consumers-in-cambodia)), and cash on delivery remains common, although no primary survey sizes it ([Trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/cambodia-ecommerce)). BizManage's order link and delivery-fee tracking fit this seller, but ability to pay is lower.

Three behavioural facts shape how every persona is reached and converted. First, discovery runs through Facebook and peers: **58% of small firms use social media for business information, and 14% rely on other business owners** ([CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)), while locally made platforms are "not widely used yet" ([ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)). Second, both platforms matter. Cambodian mobile web traffic splits roughly **55.5% Android and 44.5% iOS** ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/cambodia)), an unusually high iOS share for the region, but staff phones are likely to be cheaper Android devices, so the missing Play listing blocks persona A's staff and persona B's branches. Third, paying is itself a hurdle. Only **39% of Cambodian adults have a financial account** ([FinDev Gateway / Findex 2025](https://www.findevgateway.org/region/financial-inclusion-east-asia-and-pacific)). ABA debit cards do work for App Store and Google Play billing, and ABA Mobile sells store vouchers in the app ([ABA promotion](https://ababank.com/promotions/unlock-exclusive-perks-with-aba-visa-platinum-card); [Marketech APAC](https://marketech-apac.com/cambodias-aba-bank-reels-in-forest-interactive-as-digital-content-partner/)), so the path exists but has to be explained to buyers.

The English-speaking secondary market splits the same way. Side-hustle flippers are well served by cheap profit-per-item apps such as Flippd, StockSnap and Logflip ([App Store: Flippd](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/flippd-resellers-inventory/id1633899291)), and a team price means little to one person working alone. The better English fits are market-stall operators and small importers, and owners of two small shops, who share persona B's need for remote visibility. In the US, 87% of firms with ten or fewer employees depend on referrals ([LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/small-business-marketing-trends-report-2026/)). Merchants that Square has dropped and that are moving to Loyverse are also a realistic pool ("Square dropped us after nearly 8 years", [Loyverse App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1070865387)).

## Owners type "track stock" and "daily sales", never "landed cost"

Search behaviour separates the words owners use from the words in BizManage's feature list. On Google Play, single words carry no shop intent. "pos" completes to banks and postal services, "stock" to trading apps, "sales" to Salesforce, "cashier" and "minimart" to games, and "business manager" to Facebook's tool ([Google Play suggest](https://play.google.com/_/PlayStoreUi/data/batchexecute?rpcids=IJ4APc)). The phrases that do complete into shop intent are two- and three-word forms: "pos app", "inventory app", "sales and inventory app", "inventory and sales tracker" (present in five of seven country settings), "stock app for shop" (#2 everywhere), "daily sales record", "barcode inventory stock taking" and "income statement app" (#1 in six of seven) ([Google Play suggest](https://play.google.com/_/PlayStoreUi/data/batchexecute?rpcids=IJ4APc)). The most frequent modifiers are "free" and "offline". BizManage can honestly claim neither as a blanket statement, so expect these searchers to be price-sensitive.

Several of these clusters are winnable. The apps ranking for "inventory and sales tracker" and "daily sales profit and inventory" have 100,000-500,000 installs, not millions. "profit tracker" on Play is led by trading journals with 500 and 100 installs. No shop app appears in the top ten for "income statement app" ([Play search "profit tracker"](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=profit+tracker&c=apps&gl=KH&hl=en); [Play search "income statement app"](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=income+statement+app&c=apps&gl=KH&hl=en)). On Google Search the problem is phrased as "how to track inventory for small business", "how to record daily sales in a shop", "how to calculate profit margin for a product" and "app to track sales and expenses". Excel or Google Sheets appears in almost every completion ([Google Suggest](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&q=how%20to%20record%20daily%20sales&hl=en&gl=kh)). On YouTube the largest attention pools are spreadsheet templates, such as "Stock Control Sheet In Excel" with **2.24 million views** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cs7OVqDaLqg)), and "best free POS" listicles. "X vs Loyverse" comparisons draw 10,000-13,000 views ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3lehSAyqqyc)). The real competitor at the moment a shop starts looking is a spreadsheet, and the comparison anchor in the region is Loyverse ("loyverse alternative", "loyverse vs peddlr / zobaze / kyte") ([Google Suggest](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&q=loyverse%20vs&hl=en&gl=ph)).

BizManage's differentiator phrases have no autocomplete demand in any of the eight settings tested. These include "pos with multiple units", "buy in bulk sell per piece", "cost per unit including shipping", "pos app with staff" and "pos riel dollar" ([raw autocomplete data](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&q=inventory%20app%20for%20minimart&hl=en&gl=kh)). They map instead onto searched problems. Landed cost answers "how to calculate landed cost per unit / of imported goods from china". Staff roles answer "stop staff stealing from till". The income statement answers "how to calculate profit for my business" ([Google Suggest](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&q=how%20to%20calculate%20landed%20cost&hl=en&gl=ph)). So the differentiators belong in screenshots, descriptions and video demonstrations, not in keyword fields.

Khmer search is low-volume and uncontested. Formal Khmer app phrases return almost no autocomplete. Owners mix English nouns into Khmer: "app គ្រប់គ្រងស្តុក" (stock management app), "របៀបគ្រប់គ្រង stock" (how to manage stock) and "app កត់ត្រា ចំណូល ចំណាយ" (income-expense recording app) all complete. The everyday money phrase is ចំណូលចំណាយ (income and expenses), not ប្រាក់ចំណេញ (profit) ([YouTube suggest](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&ds=yt&q=app%20%E1%9E%82%E1%9F%92%E1%9E%9A%E1%9E%94%E1%9F%8B%E1%9E%82%E1%9F%92%E1%9E%9A%E1%9E%84&hl=km&gl=kh)). No Khmer-titled stock or POS app ranks on Play for Khmer queries ([Play search](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=%E1%9E%82%E1%9F%92%E1%9E%9A%E1%9E%94%E1%9F%8B%E1%9E%82%E1%9F%92%E1%9E%9A%E1%9E%84%E1%9E%9F%E1%9F%92%E1%9E%8F%E1%9E%BB%E1%9E%80&c=apps&gl=KH&hl=en)). Khmer explainer videos on stock apps get **16,000-18,000 views** while vendor channels get 11-263 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dXzo9CCaJ_w)). Chinese search centres on 进销存app (purchase-sales-stock app), 收银系统 (cashier system) and 库存管理app免费 (free stock management app) ([Google Suggest](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&q=%E8%BF%9B%E9%94%80%E5%AD%98&hl=zh-CN&gl=kh)). Vietnamese, Thai and Indonesian queries are deep but owned by local leaders, and BizManage has none of those languages. TikTok search could not be measured, so TikTok guidance below is inferred from YouTube and Google. ## Users punish broken promises, not prices

Across about 5,100 deduplicated store reviews of POS, inventory and bookkeeping apps, users rarely give one star just because an app costs money. They give one star when a promise breaks. The themes cluster by type of app. Card-processing tills (Square, Shopify) draw funds holds, account closures and updates that break checkout: "Square permanently closed my account… and is withholding $1,600" ([Square POS App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id335393788)). Free-first and local apps draw lag, paywalls on features that used to be free, sales pressure and stock errors. In KiotViet's 201 recent negative Vietnamese reviews, **48% cite lag, crashes or errors and about 15% cite unwanted sales calls after a trial signup**. One reviewer wrote that sales staff call "from morning to night" before purchase and support is "nowhere" after it (translated) ([KiotViet App Store VN](https://apps.apple.com/vn/app/id1146695068)). The same before-and-after pattern appears in India: "a minimum of 10 calls per day to purchase the app. And not a single call after we buy" ([Vyapar Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.android.vyapar)).

| Complaint | What it looks like | Why it matters for BizManage |
|---|---|---|
| Free feature moved behind a paywall | Loyverse limiting sales history to 31 days: "I have to now pay for last month's sales records" ([Loyverse AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id1070865387)) | Never take away what users already have; keep past data visible after a plan lapses |
| Per-device or per-salesperson licence | "my salesman cannot create invoices unless I purchase an additional license" ([Vyapar Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.android.vyapar)) | Directly answered by "whole team, one price" |
| Trial too short to see value | "The trial period of 7 days is way too short. i did 1 sale" ([Vyapar Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.android.vyapar)) | The 50-sale cap sits right next to this trigger |
| Paywall eats work in progress | A sale "suddenly disappears" behind a sync upgrade pop-up ([Vyapar Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.android.vyapar)) | Let the 51st sale finish before asking |
| Stock not deducting or not syncing | At least five Thai reviews in two weeks after Loyverse v3.12 ([Loyverse TH](https://apps.apple.com/th/app/id1070865387)) | One bad release turns loyal users into one-star reviewers |
| Data lost | "I trusted this app for 5 years" (translated) ([BukuWarung Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bukuwarung)) | Rare in volume, but the most destructive event |
| Wrong profit | "Split payments… aren't tracked accurately" (410 upvotes); "Opening stock is considered profit" (76 upvotes) ([Vyapar Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.android.vyapar)) | Low volume, high intensity: the considered buyer checks the profit maths |
| Cheap Bluetooth printers blocked on iOS | About 15 Loyverse reviews across PH, MY, TH, ID, VN, US naming Xprinter, PT-210, PeriPage ([Loyverse PH](https://apps.apple.com/ph/app/id1070865387)) | A fixable regional hardware gap |
| "Is it free?" fear; card-only subscription | "I want to try but I'm afraid of paying" (translated, TH); "Subscription payment always rejected" (translated, ID) ([Loyverse TH](https://apps.apple.com/th/app/id1070865387); [Loyverse ID](https://apps.apple.com/id/app/id1070865387)) | State the price on the listing and on day 0 |

Praise is the mirror image. "Easy" or "simple" appears in about **28% of recent five-star reviews**, ahead of human support (24 mentions), speed (19), inventory (13) and reports (12). Phone-only operation is praised in its own words ("all I need is my phone", [Square POS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id335393788)). The switching stories show when a user becomes available to a competitor: a price or plan change, a lockout from data or account, or a support case that failed after payment. Sortly reviewers describing a "900% price increase in two years" ([Sortly GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id529353551)) and Loyverse users leaving over CSV export moving behind a paywall ([Loyverse Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.loyverse.sale)) are the clearest cases. BizManage can turn each of these into a stated promise. It does not process cards, so it never holds anyone's money. The price covers the whole team. Data stays exportable. Existing users keep their price. Each promise must be kept in practice, because the review record shows that a broken implicit promise, not the price, is what produces the one-star review.

## No cheap app does what BizManage does, and its listing does not say so

The competitive map has three layers, and BizManage beats each one on a different argument. Free bank apps (ABA Merchant, Bakong, ACLEDA) show what came in, but not stock, cost of goods, profit or staff ([ABA Merchant App Store](https://apps.apple.com/kh/app/id1559488956)). Global freemium tills (Loyverse, Square, Shopify, Zoho, Sortly) are polished but charge for staff, stores or inventory depth. None handles riel and dollar at the shop's own rate ([Loyverse pricing](https://loyverse.com/pricing); [Zoho Inventory pricing](https://www.zoho.com/inventory/pricing/)). Regional players charge $10-110 per outlet and sell through field teams in their home markets, and their iOS ratings are weak: KiotViet 2.9, Sapo 3.46, StoreHub Manager 1.89 ([KiotViet App Store VN](https://apps.apple.com/vn/app/id1146695068); [StoreHub pricing](https://www.storehub.com/my/pricing/)). On price, Daily Sales Record charges exactly **$9.99 a month and $99.99 a year** for a simpler product with 4.71 stars from 1,079 US ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id415458892)), and KiotViet's Basic plan is about $10 per branch with a three-user cap ([KiotViet](https://www.kiotviet.vn/phi-dich-vu)). BizManage's price is therefore accepted, not aggressive, and its edge lies in what that price includes.

The local shelf is nearly empty. Loyverse has 26 Cambodian App Store ratings, and every local POS app found has single-digit counts. MyLekha calls itself "the a single free POS… in Cambodia" on 10 ratings ([MyLekha App Store](https://apps.apple.com/kh/app/id1576065840)). Ten honest ratings would put BizManage near the top of the local set. No competitor title or tagline claims real profit after cost of goods. Daily Sales Record's "Track your daily sales and profit in seconds" comes closest ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id415458892)). Against that opening, BizManage's own listing undercuts it in three ways. The store languages show English only, although the app ships Khmer and Chinese. The rating count is zero. And the US page still lists retired "Pro" ($4.99) and "Pro Plus" ($6.99) plans next to the $9.99 Max plan, which contradicts the one-plan message ([BizManage App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6747810928)). The main competitive risks are that Loyverse is free, already in Khmer and dominant in search, and that KiotViet lists Khmer and could expand. A bank adding inventory to a free merchant app is a third, speculative risk.

### Positioning statement

For internal use (it names no market, so it can also be adapted publicly):

> For shop owners who have hired help, opened a second branch or buy stock in bulk, and who can no longer tell from a notebook or a bank app where the money and goods went, BizManage is the phone-first POS and stock app that shows real profit after every cost and who sold what. Unlike free tills that charge per employee or per store for those features, and bank apps that only show what came in, BizManage covers the whole team and every branch for one simple price.

Public taglines, all country-neutral: "Know your real profit, not just your sales." "See where the money went." "One price. Whole team. Real profit."

### Pricing and paywall implications

Keep $9.99 and $99.99. The price is at the market rate for one branch and far below per-seat stacks, and discounting will not beat free. The risk is the gate, not the price. Against Loyverse's unlimited free core, a 50-sale cap reads as a trial. *(Corrected 15 Nov 2026: the free cap is 50 sales, not 10. This judgement was made at 10 and is weaker at 50.)* The review evidence shows that a short trial reads as "bait" unless the user has already seen the payoff. So make the owner see a profit number before the cap: show the income statement or a stock-value screen by about the fifth sale, and fire the store-review prompt at that win. State the cap on the listing and on day 0 ("Free for your first 50 sales, then one plan for the whole team"). Let the 51st sale complete, or save it as a draft, before asking. After a plan lapses, keep past records visible and exportable. Test a larger cap or a time-based trial against the current 50 sales, measuring both time to first profit view and conversion. That test is a hypothesis, not a finding. Remove the retired Pro and Pro Plus plans from the store page. Promise grandfathering and announce any plan change in the app. Keep monthly prominent for cash-flow-sensitive minimarts, and lead with yearly for multi-branch owners and importers. Keep billing inside the stores, with one-tap cancellation. Publish a short help page on paying with a local debit card or store gift cards, because many intending buyers have no card on their store account. Never follow a signup with sales calls. In KiotViet's reviews that practice alone accounts for about one in seven one-star complaints.

### What to avoid

Avoid claiming "offline". It is unverified for BizManage and would draw angry reviews from the very searchers who use the word. Avoid "free" without its condition, and avoid "invoice", "tax" and "bookkeeping" as compliance claims, since BizManage files nothing to tax portals and those are table-stakes complaints in Viet Nam, India and the Philippines. Do not lead with jargon such as landed cost, UoM or income statement as headlines. Show them as answers to owners' questions. Do not chase Viet Nam, Thailand or Indonesia before adding those languages and local tax integration. Do not build features for cafés, and do not court solo flippers with team pricing. Do not ship releases that move buttons or risk stock deductions without testing, because release discipline and safe writes protect ratings more than any new feature does. Finally, apply the no-country rule everywhere: public copy, hashtags, scripts, on-screen text, store text, web pages and the Khmer and Chinese translations, including the two ASO plan lines noted above.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves BizManage's challenge from features to proof and promises. The product already holds the combination no cheap competitor offers. What is missing is everything a buyer checks before trusting it: ratings, the language they read, a clear price and a profit number seen before the paywall. Because the paying segment is defined by a moment (the first outside hire, the second branch, the unexplained shortfall), marketing should be built around that moment rather than around shop categories. The softest, most shareable phrasing is "where did the money go", not "your staff are stealing".

The no-country rule has a real cost: it gives up local-intent searches such as "best POS system in [country]". But the Khmer language recovers most of that reach without naming anything. A Khmer store listing, Khmer-captioned video and code-mixed Khmer titles reach exactly the local buyer, on surfaces where no competitor ranks today. Banks are both the cheapest competitor and the most logical future partner. Bookkeeping data has lending value, as BukuWarung's move into financing showed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2052983)), and a stock-and-profit record is what a lender cannot get from transaction history alone. That partnership is a longer-term option, worth keeping open while the first job is to earn the first ten ratings.
