# Personas and buyer behaviour for small-shop POS/inventory apps (BizManage)

Research date: 15 November 2026. Each dated figure gives its data year. Where a source is a vendor blog, a search-engine summary or a secondary aggregator, that is stated. Two primary PDFs were read in full text: the ADB gendered-barriers report (Dec 2025, fieldwork May 2024) and the Asia Foundation CBEB 2025 key-findings brief (fieldwork Mar-May 2025).

## Segments: size, pain, current tools, ability to pay

### Takeaway
The Cambodian market is mostly very small firms. There are about 754k establishments, the median firm has 2 workers, and about 70% are owned by women. Sales/retail is the most female-owned sector, at 74%. Almost all firms are informal: 7% are registered with the tax department. Digital use stops at mobile payments and Facebook. Stock-management apps are rare, but 78% of micro owners say they need more digital tools. In the current evidence the paying core is the minority with staff and growth (segments a with staff, d, f). Solo sellers are numerous but hard to monetise.

### Cited Findings
- 2022 Economic Census: 753,670 firms in Cambodia, ~20% in Phnom Penh, then Kandal 7.5%, Siem Reap 6.7%, Battambang 6.6%. About 70% (520,474 firms) are owned by women. — [ADB, Gendered Barriers Facing MSMEs in Cambodian Cities (Dec 2025)](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- Female ownership is highest in accommodation (76%) and sales (74%) (2022 census data). — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- Average firm has 3.95 workers, median 2.00. 49% are one-person firms and 44% have 2-4 workers. Female-owned firms: 55% solo, 42% with 2-4 workers. Male-owned: 36% solo, 49% with 2-4. Women own 77% of solo firms, 66% of 2-4 worker firms and 41% of 5-9 worker firms. Female-owned firms use more unpaid family workers. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- In a primary survey of 414 MSMEs in 3 provinces (May 2024), 70% of microenterprises accepted mobile payments, and about 85% of MSMEs used no digital help in quality control. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- A key informant says owners use digital tools for payments and marketing, but "the use of mobile applications to manage their business, such as stock management, is limited." — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- 78% of microenterprise owners and over 90% of SME owners say their business needs more digital transformation. Owners who already use more digital tools want more. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- Only 1.5% of male-owned and 0.8% of female-owned firms reported adopting digital technology in response to COVID-19 (2022 census). — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- CBEB 2025 (n=2,741 MSEs, representative of more than 700k businesses, fieldwork Mar-May 2025):

  - 74% receive e-payments (83% urban, 63% rural).
  - Very few use cloud storage, chatbots or AI.
  - — [Asia Foundation, CBEB 2025 Key Findings](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)
- CBEB 2025 on demand and money:
  - 83% named declining sales a top concern in 2024, and 73% expect sales to fall in 2025.
  - High production costs were the second concern (40%).
  - 25% have a loan, and 70% of those without one say they don't need one.
  - Many MSEs were not interested in borrowing or investing more capital.
  - — [Asia Foundation CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)
- KiotViet, the Vietnam benchmark for a segment (a)/(b) mobile POS, lists an entry plan at VND 270,000/month per branch (about US$10-11), Professional at VND 330,000 and Premium at VND 490,000. It has no free plan, only a 10-day trial. Prices were checked by an aggregator on 11 Oct 2026, not the vendor page. — [softwares.com KiotViet pricing](https://www.softwares.com/software/kiotviet/pricing). The 2020 SME price was VND 180,000 (US$7.72). — [KrASIA via KiotViet blog](https://www.kr-asia.com/this-company-has-quietly-staked-a-corner-in-vietnams-mobile-pos-market-startup-stories). KiotViet says it has digitised 100,000 Vietnamese SMEs and attributes trials to affordable pricing and service. — same source

Paid add-ons are charged per store: Advanced Inventory US$25/month, Employee Management US$25/month and unlimited history US$5/month. Sources conflict on the employee add-on: its llms.txt says €5/employee/month. — [Loyverse US pricing](https://loyverse.com/en-us/pricing); [search summary of loyverse llms.txt / third-party trackers](https://loyverse.com/llms.txt)
- Segment (c), social sellers: Cambodian businesses rely on Facebook, TikTok and Telegram to sell (Xinhua via The Star, Feb 2025). — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/02/22/booming-platform-economy-offers-convenience-to-consumers-in-cambodia)
  - COD share is put at 25-26.5% of e-commerce transactions. Both figures come from commercial Shopify-app guides with no primary survey cited. — [EasySell 2026 guide](https://easysellapp.com/blogs/wiki/cambodia-ecommerce-cod-shopify-market-entry-2026)
  - A 2022 Profitence consumer survey (n=1,500) found that lack of trust drives a slight preference for COD. — [Profitence e-commerce 2022 report](https://ezstatic1.ezweb.online/ezweb_1540/html/profitence-ecomm2022report-copy.html)
  - Older reporting (2019) describes sellers who buy stock on Chinese e-commerce sites and resell it via Facebook/phone with cash on delivery. This is the segment (c) and (d) overlap. — [Rouse, 2019](https://www.rouse.com/magazine/news/cambodia-and-vietnam-s-online-shopping-industries-where-are-we-up-to/)
  - Cambodia has no national postcode or systemised addresses. — [DHL Cambodia social commerce guide](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-kh/e-commerce-advice/e-commerce-sector-guides/embracing-social-commerce-in-cambodia) (per search summary)
- Global English resellers (segment adjacent): phone-first reseller apps such as Flippd, StockSnap, SellerBook 2 and Logflip compete on profit per item after fees and shipping. Flippd's free tier allows 20 items, 5 mileage entries and 5 expenses, and it has about 73 App Store reviews. Crosslisting tools like Nifty start at US$25/month. — [App Store: Flippd](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/flippd-resellers-inventory/id1633899291); [nifty.ai](https://nifty.ai/post/app-for-resellers) (vendor content)

### Inferences
- (a) Family minimart/grocery: the largest group in Cambodia. It is mostly female-owned, with 1-4 workers, often unpaid family members. Pain points are stock outs and unknown margins. Today's tools are a notebook, memory and KHQR. Ability to pay is low until there is a non-family employee or a second outlet.
- (b) Specialist retail (phone accessories, cosmetics, clothing, pharmacy, hardware): many SKUs, variants and higher unit value, so a single missing item hurts. This segment is more likely to have hired staff and to see the point of barcode and per-staff sales. Medium ability to pay. Direct evidence is thin (see Gaps).
- (c) Social/COD sellers: very numerous. Their pain is reconciling orders, COD remittances from couriers and delivery fees, and stock that is shared between online and offline. Ability to pay is low to medium. The order-link/QR menu and delivery-fee tracking are the fit.
- (d) Importer/wholesaler: needs landed cost, carton vs piece units and margin by shipment. These firms are larger, more often male-owned (women own only 41% of 5-9 worker firms) and better placed to pay US$10/month. Highest willingness to pay per account, but fewer firms.
- (e) Cafe/small F&B: well served by Loyverse's free POS and kitchen display. BizManage's edge is weaker here unless real profit or expenses matter to the buyer.
- (f) Multi-branch absentee owner: the clearest buyer. Remote visibility and staff accountability are what they pay for. Loyverse charges per store, so a whole-team flat price works in BizManage's favour.
- Weak demand (83% saw falling sales) and low appetite for investment make "find lost money and real profit" a stronger message than "grow".

### Gaps
- No source gives the count of Cambodian firms by sub-sector: minimart vs phone shops vs pharmacy vs importers. The census sector table exists, but only the "sales" aggregate was extracted.
- No Cambodian survey of social sellers (Telegram/TikTok Shop seller counts, order volumes, COD share) was found. The COD figures come from vendor blogs.
- No data on importer/wholesaler counts or practices in Cambodia.
- No Cambodia-specific study of willingness to pay for POS software was found.

## Jobs to be done and trigger moments

### Takeaway
Evidence for triggers is mostly indirect. Hiring non-family staff is the best-evidenced driver: employee theft is a large share of retail loss in Western data. Not knowing profit is a near-universal latent pain: most micro owners keep no formal records, and records correlate with higher profit. Tax registration is a weak trigger in Cambodia (7% registered with tax, 1% plan to register). An accountant asking for records is mostly irrelevant for micro firms and relevant only for registered SMEs.

### Cited Findings
- Internal theft (employees) was 29% of US retail shrink, and external theft 36% (NRF National Retail Security Survey 2023, FY2022 data). Total shrink was US$112.1bn, average rate 1.6%. — [NRF / Appriss NRSS 2023](https://info.apprissretail.com/hubfs/Resource-Center/2023.038-national-retail-security-survey-report-2023.pdf). NRF stopped publishing an average shrink percentage after 2023. — [NRF blog](https://nrf.com/blog/the-misperception-of-shrink-and-its-impact-on-organized-retail-crime)
- A secondary source citing Appriss's 2026 benchmark says employee theft is about 29% of US shrink, about US$26bn a year. Not verified against the primary report. — [buildingsecurity.com 2026 stats](https://www.buildingsecurity.com/statistics/retail-theft)
- Claims that "85% of small retailers experience theft yearly" and that "1-10 employee retailers lose $50,000/yr to employee theft" have no traceable primary source and should not be used. — [Worldmetrics](https://worldmetrics.org/retail-employee-theft-statistics/) (unreliable)
- A vendor-cited Schulman Center claim says ~60% of inventory losses are linked to employee theft. Vendor blog, use with caution. — [search summary of POS vendor blogs](https://goftx.com/blog/how-pos-system-improve-employee-theft-prevention/)
- Theft methods relevant to the product: inflating stock counts and blaming the variance on "admin error", and skimming cash while recording a lower sale. — [DEV Community article](https://dev.to/veira_59608bdb2de0135bea1/how-theft-happens-without-owners-noticing-the-invisible-methods-costing-you-money-435g) (non-academic)
- Record keeping and profit: a study of 1,301 small retailers in urban Jakarta found that record-keeping practices are associated with higher monthly profits. — [NBER conference paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f115594.pdf)
- In rural Bohol, Philippines, 14 of 18 small businesses (77.78%) had no formal records and relied on memory (2026 case study, small n). — [IJRISS 2026](https://repository.gyaanarth.com/pdfs/ijriss/vol10-iss4-pg6608-6619-202605_pdf.pdf)
- ADB recommends that Cambodian ministries and the National Bank deliver financial education covering "financial management, and bookkeeping" for business owners. This implies bookkeeping is a recognised gap. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- Tax registration (CBEB 2025):
  - Registration with MOC rose from 6% to 10% and with GDT from 4% to 7% (2022 to 2025). By gender, 12% of male and 4% of female respondents are registered with GDT.
  - Reasons for staying informal: "too small" (73%), others aren't registered (27%), don't know how (26%).
  - Only ~1% of unregistered firms plan to register, and 80% of those don't know when.
  - 79% are unaware of the tax incentives for voluntary registration.
  - — [Asia Foundation CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)
- Tax incentives:
  - Micro firms that voluntarily register between 2023 and 2028 are exempt from registration fees, and must report annual turnover to GDT by 31 March.
  - Small taxpayers paid a US$100 patent tax for 2025 by 31 March 2025.
  - Each enterprise faces an on-site tax audit about every 3 years.
  - — [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/cambodia-grants-tax-incentives-for-investment-project-expansion-and-msmes/2/); [DFDL 2025 compliance](https://dfdl.com/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/2025/01/2025-Annual-Compliance-DFDL.pdf); [KPMG Cambodia 2025 audit programme](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/kh/pdf/technical-update/2025/english/Notifications%20on%20incorrect%20tax%20declarations%20and%20conduct%20of%20the%20tax%20audit%20program%20in%202025.pdf) (via search summaries)
- Wholesale/retail and F&B have markedly lower MOC registration than accommodation or finance (~80%). — [CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)
- In the ADB study, SME interviewees report digitising "staff management" and "income recording systems" first. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- In Vietnam, the move to mandatory e-invoices for household businesses was used as a sales trigger: KiotViet offered 30% off, from VND 6,000/day, in June 2025. — [Viet Nam News / Bizhub](https://bizhub.vietnamnews.vn/kiotviet-launches-programme-to-ease-e-invoice-switch-for-small-businesses-post377799.html)

### Inferences
- Trigger ranking for Cambodia, from strongest to weakest. This is an inference.
  1. First non-family hire, or the owner stepping away (second branch, school run, illness). Cash and stock no longer match and there is no way to check.
  2. A stock count or reorder that reveals missing goods.
  3. Online orders scaling to the point where chats and COD can't be reconciled.
  4. A second branch.
  5. An import shipment whose real cost is unclear.
  6. Tax registration or an accountant asking for records. Weak, because few register and those who do are larger.
- Vietnam's e-invoice mandate shows that regulation can be a huge trigger. If Cambodia's GDT moves micro firms toward e-invoicing or turnover reporting, that would create a similar moment. Speculative: no such Cambodian mandate for micro firms was found.
- Low study time limits learning: 72% of micro owners spend no daily time on business skills. Onboarding must deliver the first "aha" (a profit number, or a missing-stock alert) within minutes.

### Gaps
- No SEA-specific survey on staff theft or shrink in small shops was found. Theft evidence is US-based (NRF) or anecdotal.
- No primary data links "hiring first staff" or "opening a second branch" to software purchase in SEA. It is inferred from product logic and competitor positioning: Loyverse employee add-on, KiotViet multi-branch pricing.
- Could not confirm whether GDT requires bookkeeping software or records for small self-assessment taxpayers.

## Buyer vs user; gender, age, education, device

### Takeaway
The buyer is the owner, most often a woman in Cambodian retail (74% of sales firms are female-owned). Users are the owner, family members and sometimes hired staff. The "adult child who handles tech" pattern is plausible but undocumented. Women-owned firms are smaller, less registered and slightly less likely to adopt digital tech. Device share is unusual for SEA: Cambodian web traffic is close to evenly split, with iOS around 44-52%. Both platforms are essential.

### Cited Findings
- About 70% of Cambodian firms are female-owned. Women dominate solo and 2-4 worker firms, and men are more common as firm size rises. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- Other figures on women's ownership: 69.1% of 753,670 establishments (Economic Census 2022, cited by CDRI). — [CDRI blog](https://coc2025.cdri.org.kh/blogs/driving-cambodias-long-term-vision-through-women-led-and-digital-msme-development). 62% of women business owners own micro-enterprises (GADC/iDE 2025). — [GADC/iDE report](https://gadc.org.kh/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/ENG_Women-MSMEs-Intersectional-Challenges-Report.pdf) (search summary only)
- Female-owned firms were 0.65 percentage points less likely to adopt digital tech. They are constrained by household time, limited networks and limited finance. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- Men are more likely to be in paid employment (46% vs 32% for women) and women more likely to be self-employed or in family business (2021 CSES). — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- Micro owners spend an average of 15 minutes a day on business skills (small 30 minutes, medium 50 minutes), and 72% of micro owners spend none. ADB recommends short modules and recorded sessions to fit women's time constraints. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- Few staff have ICT qualifications: about 5.0% in small enterprises, lower in micro. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- 12% of male vs 4% of female respondents are GDT-registered. — [CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)
- StatCounter Cambodia, mobile-only web traffic, Aug 2026: Android 55.5%, iOS 44.5%. Mobile + tablet (Jun 2026): iOS ~52%, Android ~48%. Search-summary figures. These are web-traffic shares, biased toward urban and wealthier users, not device ownership. — [StatCounter mobile Cambodia](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/cambodia); [StatCounter mobile-tablet Cambodia](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile-tablet/cambodia)
- Cambodian adults with a financial account: 39% (Findex 2025), up from 33% in 2021 and 22% in 2017. — [FinDev Gateway / Findex 2025 regional summary](https://www.findevgateway.org/region/financial-inclusion-east-asia-and-pacific); [cambodiamarketentry / Findex 2021](https://cambodiamarketentry.com/?p=3511)

### Inferences
- Primary buyer persona: a woman owner, probably 30s-50s, running a family shop. She is time-poor and does not study tools. She decides alone or with her spouse. Male owners are over-represented in the larger importer and multi-branch segments, which are more likely to pay.
- The user is often a different person from the buyer. Role-based staff logins and per-staff sales matter because the owner wants to see what staff do without doing it herself.
- iOS share in Cambodia's urban market is far above the SEA norm, so an iPhone-first launch is not a mistake. Rural and lower-income shops will skew Android, and staff devices are likely cheaper Android phones. Inference: StatCounter does not measure ownership.

### Gaps
- No data found on owner age distribution in Cambodian micro-retail.
- No data on the "adult child handles tech" pattern in SEA. It needs primary interviews.
- Owner education levels: the census and CSES hold this, but no extracted figure.
- No device-ownership survey separating shop owners from the general population.

## Discovery, evaluation and trust; payment methods

### Takeaway
In Cambodia, Facebook is the main business information channel (58% of MSEs use social media for business info), followed by peers (14%). Awareness of government programmes is very low (about 80% unaware). Telegram, Facebook, TikTok and Instagram dominate, and local apps are "not widely used". Payment is the hard constraint. Only 39% of adults have an account, and card ownership is low, though ABA Visa debit cards exist and can pay App Store and Google Play subscriptions. Mobile/KHQR payment is near-universal for merchants (70-74%), but KHQR cannot pay App Store billing.

### Cited Findings
- 58% of MSEs use social media for business information, Facebook most of all, and 14% get it from business peers. About 80% are unaware of some or all government enterprise programmes. — [CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)
- An interviewee in the ADB study says Facebook is very popular and that "Telegram, Facebook, TikTok, and Instagram" are more popular than Cambodian-made platforms, which are "not widely used yet". — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- The ADB study notes that customers drive adoption: when consumers ask for KHQR, owners open bank accounts. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)
- US (secondary market): 87% of businesses with 10 or fewer employees depend on referrals (LocaliQ, Oct 2025). — [LocaliQ 2026 trends](https://localiq.com/blog/small-business-marketing-trends-report-2026/). Verizon 2025 (n=600 US SMBs): 69% use YouTube and 58% TikTok for business. — [Verizon 2025 State of Small Business](https://www.verizon.com/about/news/2025-state-small-business-survey)
- ABA Bank ran a Visa Platinum promotion refunding YouTube Premium paid via Google Play or the App Store with the country set to Cambodia. This shows ABA cards work for store billing. — [ABA promotion](https://ababank.com/promotions/unlock-exclusive-perks-with-aba-visa-platinum-card)
- ABA Mobile sells Google Play and iTunes vouchers in-app, which is a route for users without a card. — [Marketech APAC](https://marketech-apac.com/cambodias-aba-bank-reels-in-forest-interactive-as-digital-content-partner/). ABA's card machines issue international debit cards on the spot. — [ABA Card Machine](https://ababank.com/self-banking/card-machine/)
- Debit card ownership was 7.4% of respondents in a Findex microdata survey. The year is unclear (likely 2014 or 2017), so treat it as old. — [World Bank Microdata](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/2444/variable/F1/V78?name=q42)
- ABA PayWay is a merchant gateway for KHQR and cards that integrates with Shopify. It could serve web billing outside the app stores. — [Shopify App Store: ABA PayWay](https://apps.shopify.com/aba-payway)

### Inferences
- Discovery channels in priority order: Facebook (pages, groups, ads), word of mouth from peer shop owners, then TikTok and Telegram. YouTube tutorials probably matter for evaluation and onboarding more than for discovery. The CBEB does not split this out.
- Suppliers and banks are an untested but logical channel. ABA already partners with merchants (foodpanda, PayWay), and wholesalers see many shop owners every week.
- Trust factors (inference from the "local platforms not popular" quote and low formality): Khmer language, a reachable human on Telegram/Facebook Messenger, and seeing peers use it. Store reviews matter less in Cambodia, where few people review apps.
- Payment friction is likely a major leak between intent and paid conversion. Many owners who would pay US$10 have no card linked to their App Store or Play account. Options to test: an ABA-voucher funding guide, web checkout via ABA PayWay/KHQR, or paying a reseller agent.

### Gaps
- No Cambodian data on how shop owners find business apps specifically, such as the share from TikTok vs Facebook vs peers.
- No data on the share of Cambodian App Store/Play accounts with a payment method attached.
- No local evidence on TikTok or YouTube creator content about shop apps (not researched within budget).
- No reliable meta-SMB report for Cambodia was found.

## Price sensitivity, willingness to pay, monthly vs yearly, lifetime vs subscription

### Takeaway
There is no direct willingness-to-pay study for Cambodian shop software. Regional anchors suggest US$7-11/month per branch is the accepted price for a full mobile POS: KiotViet's entry plan is about US$10-11, and Loyverse charges US$25/store per add-on. A free core is the norm in the free-POS niche. BizManage at US$9.99 for the whole team is priced at the market and is cheaper than Loyverse once you count multiple stores or staff. Western users complain that subscriptions add up, and pay-once POS remains a niche pitch.

### Cited Findings
- KiotViet: VND 270k/330k/490k per month per branch (2026), no free plan. — [softwares.com](https://www.softwares.com/software/kiotviet/pricing). In 2020 it was VND 180k (US$7.72) for SMEs. — [KrASIA](https://www.kr-asia.com/this-company-has-quietly-staked-a-corner-in-vietnams-mobile-pos-market-startup-stories). A 2025 promotion priced it at VND 6,000/day. — [Bizhub](https://bizhub.vietnamnews.vn/kiotviet-launches-programme-to-ease-e-invoice-switch-for-small-businesses-post377799.html)
- Loyverse: the core is free. Advanced Inventory and Employee Management are US$25/month or US$250/year each, per store. Annual billing is about 17% cheaper. 14-day add-on trials need no card. — [Loyverse pricing](https://loyverse.com/en-us/pricing)
- BukuWarung (Indonesia) does not charge a subscription for bookkeeping and monetises payments instead. Its co-founder said some merchants pay for tailored features like branded invoices, and that it was still testing willingness to pay (2021). — [YourStory 2021](https://yourstory.com/2021/07/indian-entrepreneurs-indonesian-msme-fintech-startup-bukuwarung). One profile claims subscription revenue, which conflicts. — [Extruct](https://www.extruct.ai/hub/bukuwarung-com/)
- Indonesian MSMEs' intention to use digital bookkeeping is driven by ease of use and self-efficacy, not by perceived usefulness (2024 TAM study). — [IJMAE Aug 2024 via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/0bbdce8e16ed48baa4a18286158c0383)
- Pay-once vs subscription: vendor content argues a US$50/month POS costs US$3,000 over 5 years vs a US$2,000 one-time licence. Pay-once desktop POS (FlexiPOS, CashFootprint) is still marketed in 2026. — [sam4s blog Feb 2026](https://sam4scashregisters.blogspot.com/2026/02/stop-renting-your-pos-how-retailers.html); [Indie Hackers FlexiPOS](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/flexipos-point-of-sale) (vendor-leaning)
- Typical US POS software cost for one register is US$0-200/month. — [Talo](https://talo.com/costs/point-of-sale-pos-system-cost)
- In Cambodia, 70% of MSEs without a loan say they don't need one, and many don't want to invest more capital. — [CBEB 2025](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/52926_cbeb-2025-key-findings_brief.pdf)

### Inferences
- In the SEA anchor set, US$9.99/month is at the top end of what a solo micro shop will pay, and cheap for a team or multiple branches. The whole-team, all-branch flat price beats Loyverse once there are 2+ stores or staff tracking plus inventory.
- Yearly at US$99.99 (2 months free) suits the multi-branch and importer personas. Monthly suits cash-flow-sensitive minimarts. The low investment appetite seen in CBEB argues for keeping monthly prominent.
- The free 50-sale limit is very low next to Loyverse's free unlimited core and KiotViet's 10-day trial. Owners may hit the paywall before seeing value: profit or stock insight takes days of data. This is a hypothesis to test, not a finding. *(Corrected 15 Nov 2026: the free cap is 50 sales, not 10. This judgement was made at 10 and is weaker at 50.)*
- No evidence found that SEA micro owners prefer lifetime licences. The objection is more likely "free apps exist" (Loyverse, BukuWarung) than "I want to own it".

### Gaps
- No Cambodian or SEA survey of monthly vs annual preference or of willingness to pay for POS software.
- No Reddit primary threads were retrieved. Search returned only vendor content, so Western attitudes to subscriptions vs lifetime licences are supported only by vendor blogs.

## Theft/shrinkage and owner absence as purchase drivers

### Takeaway
Employee theft is a large, well-documented share of retail loss in the US: about 29% of shrink, US$26bn. Small firms are more exposed to cash skimming. No SEA quantification exists. The product mechanisms that address it (per-staff sales, voids/returns audit, stock counts with variance) match the documented theft methods: skimming, under-ringing, inflated counts.

### Cited Findings
- Internal/employee theft accounts for 29% of US retail shrink (NRSS 2023, FY2022). — [NRSS 2023](https://info.apprissretail.com/hubfs/Resource-Center/2023.038-national-retail-security-survey-report-2023.pdf)
- About US$26bn a year (secondary citation of Appriss 2026). — [buildingsecurity.com](https://www.buildingsecurity.com/statistics/retail-theft)
- Cash theft and payroll/cheque tampering are more prevalent in smaller firms (US vendor survey, per search summary). — [Software Advice: inventory software and employee theft](https://www.softwareadvice.com/resources/inventory-software-employee-theft-prevention/)
- Documented methods: skimming cash while recording lower sales, and inflating stock counts and blaming "admin error". — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/veira_59608bdb2de0135bea1/how-theft-happens-without-owners-noticing-the-invisible-methods-costing-you-money-435g) (non-academic)
- Loyverse monetises exactly this need: per-staff sales tracking, clock-in and access levels sit behind a paid Employee Management add-on, and a marketplace "POS Detective" app sells fraud alerts. — [Loyverse pricing](https://loyverse.com/en-us/pricing); [Loyverse marketplace POS Detective](https://loyverse.com/marketplace/pos-detective)
- In the 2022 census, female-owned Cambodian firms rely more on unpaid family workers. — [ADB 2025](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1103356/gendered-barriers-facing-msmes-cambodian-cities.pdf)

### Inferences
- The theft motive applies once a shop hires outside the family. With 49% solo firms and heavy family labour, the theft-driven buyer is a minority, but a high-intent one: shops with 3+ workers, specialist retail, multi-branch.
- The competitor's paid features show the market pays for staff accountability. BizManage includes it in the base price, which is a clear differentiator to message.
- In family-run shops, "where did the stock go" often covers spoilage, giving goods to relatives and miscounting, not only theft. Messaging about "missing stock" and "real profit" is less accusatory than "staff theft". Inference.

### Gaps
- No SEA or Cambodia shrink or employee-theft data found.
- No study linking a theft incident to a POS purchase.

## Global English secondary market personas

### Takeaway
English-speaking micro-sellers split into two groups. Resellers and flippers (eBay, Poshmark, Depop, sneakers, thrift) are served by many cheap phone-first "profit per item" apps. Micro retail and market-stall sellers are served by Square, Shopify POS and Loyverse. Both depend on referrals. BizManage's distinctive features (multi-unit cartons, landed cost, multi-branch, staff roles) suit small importers and multi-stall operators more than single-item flippers.

### Cited Findings
- The reseller app category is crowded with phone-only profit trackers: Flippd (profit after fees and shipping, free tier 20 items), StockSnap (COGS and margin per item), SellerBook 2, Logflip (eBay/Poshmark/Depop/FB Marketplace), ResellStats, Flow. Many are new with few ratings. — [App Store: Flippd](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/flippd/id1633899291); [App Store: StockSnap](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6762114967); [App Store: Logflip](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6742422041); [App Store: SellerBook 2](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6745430211)
- Reddit users praise crosslister Nifty (from US$25/month) over Vendoo and criticise Vendoo bugs (via a vendor roundup, not primary Reddit). — [nifty.ai](https://nifty.ai/post/app-for-resellers)
- 87% of US firms with ≤10 employees depend on referrals. — [LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/small-business-marketing-trends-report-2026/)
- Loyverse's free core is widely reviewed in English markets, which makes it the default free benchmark. — [Merchant Maverick Loyverse review](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/reviews/loyverse-review/)

### Inferences
- Persona G1, "side-hustle flipper": one person, profit per item, crosslisting. Strong existing tools, low need for staff or branches, and it fits poorly with US$9.99 team pricing.
- Persona G2, "market stall / pop-up / small importer": cash plus card, cartons vs pieces, landed cost from overseas suppliers, one or two helpers. A better fit, though Square's free POS is the incumbent.
- Persona G3, "owner of two small shops": remote visibility and staff accountability. Same driver as SEA segment (f), and the strongest English-market fit.

### Gaps
- No primary Reddit threads (r/smallbusiness, r/Flipping, r/sneakers) were retrieved within budget. Pain-point verbatims need a dedicated Reddit mining pass.
- No US/UK/AU micro-retail size data (for example US Census nonemployer counts) was gathered.
